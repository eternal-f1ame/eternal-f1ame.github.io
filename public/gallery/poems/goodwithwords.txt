I am good with words.
The words of war, maybe not the words of love.
I am good with words.

“Hey, that is such a cool shirt,” she says.
“Umm… uh… thank you. Appreciate tha…” I mumble.
Oh, what a fumble.
I am good with shirts.

And I am good with words,
the words of power over the words of love.

Truth is, I am afraid. I am strong and loud;
instead of the person, I would rather address the crowd.
I am good with herds.

When I speak, I am heard,
so I try to be polite,
hoping I hide my plight—
swapping “oh, okay”s
for “SHUT THE FUCK UP”s,
and “hey, how are you”s
when I should be confessing my love.
I am good with words.

To ones who know me
it should come as no surprise:
astute look, intimidating eyes,
and my messy hair. I’m self-aware.
It’s beyond repair.

So I adorn my thorns, I jest and goof,
with the words unsaid piling the roof.
It collapses.

For what its worth,
I am great with words
not said by me.
I hear and listen and I know the feel,
I know the response before it’s made, all the same.
Lo and behold, what a stupid game.

I have seen the greats, too many and some.
Am not one, my only greatness is to overcome.
I know to lead (there’s no what and why),
caged bird, afraid of the open skies.
But I am good with birds,
I think.

Oh, and how I find myself here again,
talking of crowds after crying my pain.
What a shame.
Shirts, birds, herds, words: that’s all so lame.

I am good with words?
Maybe I am good without them.
